Sorry messages

A real apology has three parts: what I did, that I am sorry, and what I will do now. It has no "but". These messages follow that shape. Change the details to match what happened.

To a friend

  • I am sorry I was not there when you needed me. You deserved better from me. I would like to make it right.

  • I said something careless yesterday and it hurt you. I am sorry. It will not happen again.

  • Sorry for cancelling at the last minute, again. Your time matters. Let me plan the next one, and I will be early.

  • I forgot your birthday, and I have no good excuse. I am sorry. Can I take you out this week?

  • I should have listened and I kept talking. I am sorry. Tell me again. This time I will hear you.

To a partner

  • I am sorry for raising my voice. Whatever the argument was, you did not deserve that tone.

  • I was wrong, and I knew it while I was arguing. I am sorry. Can we talk this evening?

  • Sorry I have been on my phone more than with you. I noticed. I am putting it down.

  • I am sorry I forgot what you asked me to do. It was important to you, so it should have been important to me.

To parents and family

  • Sorry, Maa. I spoke rudely this morning. I was stressed, and I took it out on you. That was wrong.

  • Papa, I am sorry I did not call for so long. I have no excuse. I will call every Sunday from now on.

  • I am sorry I missed the family function. I know it mattered. I will come home next weekend.

  • Sorry, didi. I borrowed it without asking and I should have asked. I will replace it.

At work

  • I apologise for the delay in sending the report. It was my oversight. You will have it by 5 pm today.

  • I am sorry for the mistake in the file. I have corrected it and checked the rest. The revised copy is attached.

  • Apologies for missing the meeting. I have read the notes and will complete my action points by tomorrow.

  • We are sorry for the trouble with your order. We are fixing it today and will update you by evening.

  • I apologise for my tone in the meeting. Your point was fair, and I should have heard it out.

Why most apologies fail

Because they are really defences. "Sorry, but you also..." and "Sorry if you were hurt" both tell the other person that you think they are the problem. A real apology gives up the argument for the length of one message. You can discuss who else was wrong another day. If the apology is sincere, that day often never needs to come.

Worked example

  • Weak: "Sorry yaar, I was busy."
  • Better: "I am sorry I missed your call on your birthday."
  • Best: "I am sorry I missed your birthday. It was careless of me, and you deserved better. Can I take you to dinner on Saturday?"

Message or in person?

A message is fine for small things and as a first step for large ones. For a serious hurt, say it face to face or on a call, where the other person can hear your voice. Send the message first only to ask: "I owe you an apology. Can I call you this evening?"

After you send it

  • Do not ask "are we okay now?" an hour later.
  • Do what you said you would do. The changed behaviour is the real apology.
  • If they do not reply, wait some days and try once more, briefly. Then leave the door open and stop knocking.

Make it yours

  • Name the mistake. "I am sorry I forgot your birthday" works. "Sorry if you felt bad" does not.
  • Do not explain in the same message. Explanations sound like excuses. Offer them later if asked.
  • Give the person time. An apology is not a demand to be forgiven.
