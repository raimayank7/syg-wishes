Friendship messages

Friends rarely tell each other what they mean to each other. These messages do it lightly, without making it awkward.

For a best friend

  • You know all my stories, and you still ask "then what happened?" That is friendship.

  • Thank you for being the person I do not have to perform for.

  • You are the first person I want to tell good news to, and bad news too.

  • We have not changed. We have only become more expensive versions of the same fools.

  • Friends like you make the hard years shorter.

For an old friend

  • I found our old class photo today. Same faces in my head, even now. How are you?

  • It has been years, and I would still know your laugh in a crowd. Let us talk soon.

  • No reason for this message, except that a good memory of us came up. Hope you are well.

  • We lost touch, not the friendship. I am picking it up from here. Hello again.

For a friend who helped

  • You did not ask questions. You just came. I will remember that all my life.

  • Some people give advice. You gave time. Thank you, my friend.

  • When things went wrong, your number was the one I dialled. Thank you for picking up.

Friends go unsaid

We thank waiters and strangers more often than we thank our closest friends. The friendship is assumed, like the floor. A message that says it out loud, once in a while, costs a minute and is remembered for years, especially by friends who seem not to need it.

Three kinds of friend, three kinds of message

  • The daily friend: keep it light. A joke with a real compliment hidden in it.
  • The old friend you lost touch with: no apology for the gap, no long catching up. "Saw a red cycle just like yours today and thought of you. How are you?"
  • The friend who stood by you: say exactly what they did, and that you have not forgotten.

Make it yours

Use the nickname. Mention the place: the canteen, the coaching centre, the cricket ground, the hostel mess. One shared noun does more than three adjectives. Then end with a plan, even a small one, because friendships do not fade from quarrels. They fade from postponed plans.

What to avoid

  • Guilt: "You never call." Call, if you want to talk.
  • Comparing: "my only real friend" wounds the others.
  • Group forwards on Friendship Day. One line written for one person beats them all. Greetings for the day itself are on Friendship Day wishes.

Make it yours

  • For an old friend you lost touch with, do not explain the gap. Just say hello.
  • A message that ends with a plan keeps a friendship going.
