Polite reminder messages

A reminder works when it is easy to act on: say what is pending, the amount or the item, the date you need it by, and how to complete it. Stay polite. The first reminder should assume the person simply forgot.

Payment from a customer

  • Hello [Name], a gentle reminder that invoice [number] for Rs [amount] was due on [date]. Kindly arrange the payment at your convenience. Please ignore this if already paid.

  • Dear [Name], this is a follow up on the pending payment of Rs [amount] against invoice [number]. Could you please confirm the date by which it will be cleared? Thank you.

  • Dear [Name], the payment of Rs [amount] is now [number] days overdue. We request you to clear it by [date] so that we can continue the supply without a break.

Money lent to a friend or relative

  • Hi [Name], hope all is well. A small reminder about the Rs [amount] from last month. Let me know when it is convenient. No hurry this week.

  • Hey, I have a few payments coming up. Could you return the Rs [amount] by [date]? UPI works fine. Thanks, yaar.

Rent, fees and society dues

  • Hello [Name], this is a reminder that the rent for [month] is due on [date]. Kindly transfer it as usual. Thank you.

  • Dear parents, the tuition fee for [month] is due by [date]. Please ignore this message if you have already paid.

  • Dear residents, the maintenance for this quarter is due by [date]. Please pay at the office or by UPI and share the screenshot with your flat number.

Pending work or reply

  • Hi [Name], just following up on my mail of [date] about [topic]. Could you share an update when you get a moment?

  • Hello [Name], a quick reminder that the documents are due today. Please send them by 5 pm so that we can file on time.

  • Hi team, the report is due tomorrow. If anyone is stuck, tell me today so we can sort it out.

  • Gentle reminder about our meeting at [time] today. The link is the same as last time.

Assume they forgot

Most late payments and pending replies are forgetfulness, not bad intent. A first reminder that says so, "this may have slipped your mind", lets the other person fix it without losing face, and that is the quickest way to get what you want. Save firmness for the third message.

Three steps

  • Gentle: a friendly nudge with the details, a few days after the due date.
  • Clear: a week later. State the amount, the original date and a new date you need it by.
  • Final: say plainly what you will do next, such as pausing supplies, adding the late fee already agreed, or referring it to the society committee. Then do exactly that.

Make it easy to act

Put everything needed in the message itself: the bill or invoice number, the amount, the date, and how to pay. If you share payment details, check them twice. For the person paying: confirm the name shown on the payment screen before you send money, and never pay to a number or link that arrives from an unknown sender claiming to be someone you know.

Money between friends and relatives

This is the hardest reminder of all. Do it in private, never in a family group. Be direct and kind: "I need the amount back by the 10th for the school fees. Will that be possible?" Offering instalments often gets things moving. Keep a short written record of what was lent and returned. It protects the friendship as much as the money.

For anything serious

For large sums, disputed bills or repeated default, a message is only a record. Advice from a lawyer, a chartered accountant or the consumer forum is the proper next step, and they will tell you what kind of formal notice is needed.

Make it yours

  • Give one clear date. "By Friday" works better than "at the earliest".
  • Include what they need to act: invoice number, amount, UPI ID or account details.
  • Raise the firmness slowly: gentle first, clear second, final third.
