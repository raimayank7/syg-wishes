Condolence messages

Nothing you write will fix a loss, and it does not need to. A condolence message only has to say: I heard, I am sorry, I am here. Keep it short and plain.

These messages avoid saying how the family "should" feel. Add the name of the person who died and, if you can, one good memory of them.

General

  • I am so sorry for your loss. I am thinking of you and your family.

  • I heard the sad news. No words are enough. Please know that I am with you.

  • My deepest condolences. May you find strength in the love of the people around you.

  • I am sorry. There is no need to reply. I just wanted you to know I am here.

  • We are heartbroken to hear the news. Our prayers are with your whole family.

Loss of a parent

  • I am very sorry about your father. He was a good man, and he was proud of you. I saw it every time he spoke of you.

  • Your mother's kindness touched everyone who came to your home. I am so sorry for your loss.

  • Losing a parent is losing a part of home. I am sorry. I am here for whatever you need.

  • Uncle always asked about my studies and remembered the answers. I will miss him. My condolences to all of you.

Loss of a spouse or child

  • I cannot imagine what you are going through. I am here, today and in the months ahead.

  • There are no words for a loss like this. We are holding your family in our prayers.

For a colleague or acquaintance

  • Please accept my sincere condolences on your loss. Take all the time you need. Work will be looked after.

  • Our whole team is sorry for your loss. We are thinking of you and your family.

A simple shape that never fails

Four short parts: that you heard, that you are sorry, one true thing about the person who died, and that you are there. "I just heard about Uncle. I am so sorry. He always asked about my studies and remembered the answers. I am here for anything you need." You do not have to be eloquent. You have to be present.

Timing

  • Send a short message as soon as you hear. Do not wait to find perfect words.
  • Do not expect a reply, and do not call repeatedly in the first days.
  • The harder time comes later, when the visitors have gone. A message after two weeks, or on the first festival or birthday without the person, is the one families remember.

Faith and custom

Families mourn in different ways, and every faith has its own words and rites. If you share the family's tradition, the customary phrase is right and comforting. If you do not, or are not sure, plain words of sorrow are always respectful: "I am so sorry for your loss. He will be remembered." Avoid explaining the death or what happens after it. That belongs to the family's own belief.

Practical help

Grief makes ordinary tasks hard. Offer something exact and small: food for a day, fetching relatives from the station, minding the children during the ceremonies, handling a bank or office errand later. Then do it without being reminded.

Make it yours

  • Use the person's name. "Uncle was always kind to me" comforts more than general words.
  • Avoid "be strong", "it was for the best" and "at least". Let people grieve.
  • Offer something exact: "I will bring dinner on Thursday" is better than "tell me if you need anything".
  • In a group, one short line is enough. Send the longer message privately.

Questions

Is it all right to send condolences on WhatsApp?

Yes. A short, sincere message is better than silence. Follow it with a call or a visit when the family is ready.

What should I not write?

Do not ask how it happened, do not give advice, and do not compare it with your own loss.
