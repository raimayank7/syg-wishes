Captions for festival and function photos

Festival photos come in a flood: everyone posts on the same evening. A caption that names your own small detail stands out from a hundred "festive vibes". The rangoli that took three hours, the diya your niece painted, the dupatta borrowed from your sister, the phulkari that was your grandmother's.

Make it yours

  • Name the occasion and whose it is: "Veera's wedding, day two."
  • Credit the hands: who tied the turban, who did the mehndi, who stitched the suit.
  • Add the family tradition: the first diya at the door, the sweets sent to neighbours.

Respect for the occasion

Festivals are sacred to the people who keep them. Keep captions warm and free of jokes about rituals, your own faith's or anyone else's. When you post from another community's celebration, a line of thanks to your hosts is the right note. For greetings to send on the day itself, see Wishes.

What to avoid

  • Showing off jewellery with the location switched on.
  • Posting a bride's or groom's photos before the family has shared theirs.
  • Captions about cost.

Before you post

  • Credit the person who draped the saree or tied the turban.
