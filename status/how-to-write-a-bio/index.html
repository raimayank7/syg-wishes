How to write a bio for WhatsApp and Instagram

A bio, or the About line on WhatsApp, is the first thing a new contact reads about you. It is short, it sits under your name for months, and most people write it once in a hurry. Ten minutes of thought gives you a line you will not want to change.

Start with three facts

Take a paper and write three plain facts: what you do, where you are from or live, and one small thing you love. For example: "I teach maths at a school in Patiala. I love old Hindi songs." Now cut every word that is not needed.

Maths teacher. Patiala. Old songs on repeat.

That is a finished bio. It is true, it is short, and nobody else has it. Clever lines copied from a list are shared by thousands of profiles. Your three facts are shared by none.

Four shapes that always work

Three wordsWork, place, loveNurse. Ludhiana. Chai.

WhatsApp About and Instagram bio are different jobs

Your WhatsApp About is read by people who already have your number: family, customers, the plumber, your child's teacher. Keep it plain and useful. If you run a business from that number, your timing or trade is the best About you can have.

An Instagram bio is read by strangers deciding whether to follow you. It can be lighter, but it should still answer "who is this?" in one glance. If your account is about one thing, such as baking, bikes or your shop, say that thing first.

Bios for different people

  • Students: course or class, plus one interest. "B.Sc. Nursing, second year. Badminton after five."
  • Job seekers: your field and what you are looking for, plainly. "Civil engineer. Open to site work in Punjab."
  • Shops and home businesses: what you sell, your town, how to order. Save jokes for a personal profile.
  • Teachers and doctors: your subject or speciality and your timing for calls.
  • Parents and grandparents: family lines read warmly. "Nani to three. Busy knitting."

Writing it in Hindi or Punjabi

A bio in your own language often sounds more like you. Write it in the real script, Devanagari or Gurmukhi, since it is easier to read than Hindi or Punjabi spelt in English letters. Keep it just as short: "ਅਧਿਆਪਕ। ਪਟਿਆਲਾ। ਚਾਹ ਦਾ ਸ਼ੌਕੀਨ।" is three facts, the same as in English.

What to keep out

  • Your full date of birth, home address or, on public profiles, your phone number.
  • Your child's name and school together.
  • Long rows of emoji and symbols. They push the real words off the screen and are read out one by one by screen readers.
  • Lines aimed at one person. A bio is a signboard, not a message.
  • Claims you cannot support, such as titles, degrees or "number one" anything.

Check it on the phone

After saving, look at your own profile from a friend's phone, or in the chat list. If the line is cut off with dots, shorten it until the whole thing shows. Apps change their limits from time to time, so the screen is the only reliable test.
