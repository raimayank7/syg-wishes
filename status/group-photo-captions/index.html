Captions for more than one face

A group photo belongs to everyone in it, so the caption should too. Say what brought you together, such as the wedding, the reunion, the farewell or the Sunday lunch, and leave out anything that singles one person out for teasing. The classic safe formula is the occasion, a count and a small joke: "Batch of 2012. Twelve came. Nobody has grown up."

Ask before you post

Someone in the photo always blinked. Before posting, send the photo to the group and ask if everyone is fine with it. It takes a minute and saves a friendship. Be extra careful with photos that include other people's children, or colleagues at an office party.

Make it yours

  • Name the place and year. Old friends love a date.
  • Use the group's own name or joke, without explaining it.
  • Mention who is missing: "Missing Gurpreet, who chose to have a job in Canada."

What to avoid

  • Cropping one person out and posting the rest.
  • Captions that rank the group: "my real friends", "the best cousins".
  • Tagging people who keep their profiles private.

Before you post

  • Name the occasion and the year. In ten years you will thank yourself.
