Motivational status

Motivation that works is practical. It points at the next small step. These lines are written for people who have work to do today.

English

  • Begin clumsy. Tidy up as you go.

  • Do the small task. The big one is made of small ones.

  • Keep the promise you made on a good day.

  • You do not need more time. You need fewer tabs open.

  • Tired is fine. Quitting is different.

  • One page a day is a book in a year.

  • Nobody saw the practice. Everybody will see the result.

  • Fall seven times. Take notes eight times.

  • Your future self is watching what you do after dinner.

  • The mood to work arrives after you begin, not before.

  • Hard work is boring. That is exactly why it works.

  • Be the person who finishes.

  • The plan in your head needs a date in your diary.

  • Progress is quiet. Keep going.

  • Do it badly first. Then do it better.

  • I am not there yet, but I am not where I started.

  • Do the boring part. That is where it is won.

  • Start small. Start scared. Start anyway.

  • Ten minutes today beats two hours "someday".

  • Tired is fine. Stopping is the only problem.

  • Nobody claps for practice. Practise anyway.

  • The work does not care about my mood. Good. Neither will the result.

Who are you motivating?

A motivational status usually has one real reader: you. Posting it is a small public promise, and that is why it works. So pick a line that matches what you are doing this month, such as preparing for an exam, building a shop, getting back to walking or learning to drive. A line about mountains means little if your mountain is a syllabus.

Make it specific

  • Add the count: "Day 12 of 90. Still showing up."
  • Add the task: "One chapter before breakfast."
  • Add the reason: "For the result my parents are waiting for."

These small additions turn a poster line into a diary line, and friends cheer for a diary.

What to avoid

  • Promises of quick money or overnight success. Real work is slow, and the line should admit it.
  • Lines that shame rest. Sleep and Sundays are part of the work.
  • Health claims. A status can encourage a walk. It cannot promise a cure.
  • Posting five in a row. One line, followed by the work, is stronger.

Before you post

  • Pick one line and act on it today. A status changes nothing by itself.
  • Post it for yourself. If it helps one more person, that is a bonus.
