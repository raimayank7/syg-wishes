How to write a good status, and post it safely

A status is seen for a second or two by people scrolling through many. These simple habits make yours worth that second, and keep you safe while you post.

What makes a good status

  • One line, one thought. If it needs a "read more", it is a post, not a status.
  • True for you today. A line that matches your real mood reads differently from a borrowed pose.
  • Specific beats grand. "Maa's rajma, after six months" beats any line about happiness.
  • Kind. A status aimed at one person is read by everyone else too.

How long should it be?

As a working rule, keep a text status to one or two short lines so that it can be read at a glance, and keep a profile bio short enough to show fully without tapping. Apps change their exact limits from time to time, so check inside the app if a line is being cut off.

Stay safe when you post

  • Check who can see your status in the app's privacy settings before posting family photos.
  • Do not post live locations, boarding passes, tickets, ID cards or anything with a QR code or number on it.
  • Holiday photos are safer posted after you are back home.
  • Ask before posting other people's children, and before posting a friend's bad photo.
  • Keep phone numbers, addresses and birth dates out of public bios.

Posting when you feel low

It is fine to say a day was heavy. Avoid lines that blame or hint at one person. They invite arguments. If the low mood has lasted for weeks, a status cannot carry it: talk to someone you trust, or to a doctor or counsellor.

Text status or photo status?

A text status suits a thought, a mood or a notice such as "Exam mode on, back on the 14th". A photo status suits a moment: the plate, the sky, the gathering. Do not put a long line on top of a busy photo, because neither will be read. If the words matter, use a plain background. If the photo matters, keep the words to five or six.

A ten-second check before you post

  1. Is it true for me today?
  2. Would I be comfortable if my parents, my manager and my neighbour all read it?
  3. Is it about me, or is it aimed at someone?
  4. Does the photo show anything private: a face that did not agree, an address, a document?
  5. Will I still be fine with it tomorrow morning?

Five yeses, and it is ready. One no, and it is worth a second look.

How often to post

There is no rule, but there is a pattern: people who post once a day or less get read, and people who post fifteen times a day get muted. Choose the best moment of the day and let the others stay yours.

Make a line your own

Take any line from this site and change one word to something from your life: your city, your friend's nickname, your favourite snack. "Rain outside, chai inside" becomes "Rain outside, Amma's filter coffee inside", and now nobody else has that status.
