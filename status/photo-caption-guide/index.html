How to write a caption for any photo

The photo is ready and the caption box is empty. This guide gives you a method that works for any photo in under a minute, with examples.

The one rule

A caption should add what the photo cannot show. The photo shows what things looked like. It cannot show where you were, what happened just before, what it smelt like, who took the picture or what you were thinking. Any one of those is your caption.

Five questions that write the caption for you

  1. Where and when? "Kasauli, 6.40 pm." Often this is enough on its own.
  2. What just happened? "Ten minutes after the result."
  3. Who is missing or behind the camera? "Photo by Papa, who cut off everyone's feet."
  4. What can the photo not show? "It was two degrees and we were pretending otherwise."
  5. What is the honest joke? "Smiling because this was the twelfth try."

Answer one question in a few words. Do not answer all five.

Before and after

Here is the same photo, a family at a hill station, captioned four ways.

  • Weak: "Family time." The photo already says this.
  • Better: "Mussoorie with the family."
  • Good: "Mussoorie. First trip with Dadi in twelve years."
  • Best: "Dadi's first hill station at 74. She wants to come back in winter."

Each step adds one true detail. None adds a clever phrase.

How long?

One line is ideal, and two is the limit for most photos. If you have a longer story, tell it properly as a post or a message, and do not squeeze it under a picture where it will be cut off after the first line.

Hashtags, emoji and tags

  • A few relevant hashtags at the end are fine. A wall of them looks like an advertisement.
  • One or two emoji that match the mood are enough. They should decorate the words, not replace them.
  • Tag people only if they are happy to be tagged, and never tag a location you are still at with your family.

Check the photo before the caption

  • Is everyone in it happy to be posted? Ask in the group first.
  • Is there an address, number plate, ticket, ID card or screen visible in the background?
  • Are other people's children in it? Ask their parents.
