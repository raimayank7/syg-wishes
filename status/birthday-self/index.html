Posting on your own birthday

A birthday status has two jobs: to let people know without asking for wishes, and to thank them afterwards. A light line in the morning does the first. A warm line at night does the second, and saves you from replying "thank you" a hundred times, though the people who called deserve a personal reply.

Make it yours

  • Use the age if you are happy to: "34. Knees disagree, heart is 22."
  • Mention the tradition: the kheer, the temple or gurdwara visit, the cake cut at midnight by friends who would not leave.
  • Thank by name the people who made the day.

A privacy note

Wishing yourself a happy birthday in public is harmless. Posting your full date of birth with the year is less so, since banks and many services use it as a check. A status can say "it is my birthday" without saying the year, and a public bio does not need the date at all.

What to avoid

  • Hints about gifts.
  • Keeping score of who wished and who forgot, especially in public.
  • Sad lines about growing old, unless they are clearly a joke.

Before you post

  • A thank you status at the end of the day covers everyone you could not reply to.
