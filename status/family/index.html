Good moments for a family status

A parent's birthday, a festival at home, the day you travel back, a grandparent's story, a sibling's result. Family lines are also the ones elders in your contact list enjoy most, so plain words work better than clever ones.

Write it the way your family talks

Use the names you use at home: Maa, Amma, Bebe, Papa, Daddy ji, Nani. Mention the real thing they do, such as the tiffin, the 9 pm call or the lecture about sweaters. "Papa still checks my tyres" is a whole portrait in five words. Lines in Hindi or Punjabi often land better with parents than English ones, so pick the language they read most easily.

Think before posting family photos

  • Check who can see your status before posting photos of children.
  • Ask elders if they are comfortable. Some are not, and will not say so afterwards.
  • Keep house numbers, school uniforms with names and car number plates out of the frame.

What to avoid

  • Praising one relative in a way that slights another.
  • Family disagreements, even as hints.
  • Posting only on special days. A line on a normal Tuesday means more.

Before you post

  • Parents rarely see a status. Read the line out to them too.
