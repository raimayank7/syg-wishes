Festival get-together invitation wording

Festivals are the easiest reason to bring people together. These invitations are for a Diwali card party or dinner, a Lohri bonfire, a Holi morning, an Eid lunch, a Christmas tea or a New Year evening. Type the festival name once to fill the general cards.

What to include

  • Which festival and what kind of gathering: dinner, card party, bonfire, lunch.
  • Date and time. Festival dates change every year, so write the date, not just "on Diwali".
  • Address.
  • Anything guests should bring or wear.

Tips

  • Festival evenings fill up quickly. Invite two to three weeks ahead.
  • For Holi, tell guests which colours you use and offer a place to wash.
  • For a first Lohri of a baby or a newly married couple, say so in the first line. It is a special Lohri.

Questions people ask

What is a first Lohri?

In Punjab and nearby regions, the first Lohri after a wedding or after the birth of a child is celebrated with a bigger gathering, a bonfire and gifts of rewri, gajak and peanuts.

Should a festival invitation mention food habits?

It helps. A line like "Vegetarian dinner" or "Tell us if you have any food restriction" saves both sides from awkward moments.
