Inviting on WhatsApp

Most invitations in India now travel by phone. A WhatsApp invitation is quick and free, but it can also feel careless if it is done badly. These habits keep it personal.

Personal message, group or broadcast?

  • Personal message: the best choice for family, elders and close friends. Add the person's name in the first line.
  • Group message: fine for a class, an office team or a housing society where everyone in the group is invited. Never use a group when only some members are invited.
  • Broadcast list: sends the same message privately to many people at once. Remember that a broadcast reaches only those who have saved your number in their phone.

Send a picture and the text

A picture card looks good in the chat, but text can be searched, copied into a calendar and read by people with weak eyesight. Send the card first and the details as text right after it. You can make the picture in our card maker.

Add a location pin

Send the map location as a separate message just below the invitation. Guests will look for it on the day, so keep it close to the card in the chat.

Choose the time

Send invitations in the morning or early evening, not late at night. A message that arrives at midnight gets buried by morning.

Elders first, and by voice

Call or visit the elders of the family before the message goes out. Many still feel that an invitation is real only when it is spoken. A two-minute call costs nothing and is remembered for years.

Send it fresh, not forwarded

A message marked "Forwarded" tells the guest they were an afterthought. Paste the text and attach the picture again for each person or list.

Small things that matter

  • Start with a greeting and the person's name.
  • Keep the message short enough to read without scrolling.
  • Use two or three emoji at most.
  • Reply to every "Congratulations" with at least a thank you.
  • Send one reminder a day or two before, not five.

A message that works

Here is a personal invitation message you can copy and change. It has a greeting, the name, the occasion, the day, time and place, and a line asking for a reply.

Namaste Sunita aunty, with great joy we invite you and the family to Aarav's first birthday. Sunday, 14 February 2027, 12 noon, at Hotel Park View, Mall Road (location below). Lunch will follow. It would mean a lot to have your blessings. Please let me know how many of you will come. Neha

Send the picture card first, this text second and the location pin third. More ready wording for every occasion is on the home page.

Keeping track of replies

  • Star or pin the chats that have not answered, and follow up once after a few days.
  • Keep a simple list on paper or in a notes app: name, how many are coming, any special need.
  • For a big function, ask one cousin on each side to collect replies, and give their number in the invitation.

A group for the event

For weddings and large functions, an announcement group helps: only the hosts can post, and guests receive the programme, the location pins and any change of time in one place. Tell people what the group is for when you add them, do not add anyone who has not been invited personally first, and close the group a few days after the function with a thank-you and a few photographs.

Think before posting it as a status

An invitation posted as a status is seen by every contact who views it, including people you did not mean to invite, and it shows your home address and the hours your house will be empty. Share invitations as messages to the people invited, and keep statuses for photographs after the event.
