Good night wishes

A good night message closes the day softly. Send one to the person who had a hard day, to a child away from home, or to the family group before the lights go off.

For family and friends

  • Good night. Put the day down. You carried it well.

  • Good night! Whatever is left can wait for the morning. It is very good at waiting.

  • Sleep well. Tomorrow is already on its way, and it is bringing a fresh start.

  • Good night, everyone. Phones down, lights off, worries on silent.

  • Good night, my friend. Today is done. You did enough.

  • The stars are on duty now. You can rest. Good night.

  • Good night. May you fall asleep faster than you scroll.

For someone special

  • Good night, love. The day was long, but it ended with you, so it ended well.

  • Good night. I am keeping a place for you in my dreams, as usual.

  • Sleep well, my dear. I will be the first message you see in the morning.

  • Good night. If the moon looks bright tonight, it is because I asked it to keep an eye on you.

For children

  • Good night, little one. Dream of big things. We are right here.

  • Good night, champ. You were brave today. Tomorrow we play again.

  • Sweet dreams, beta. The whole house loves you. Now close those eyes.

The last message of the day

A good night message is less a greeting than a check: are you home, are you all right, is the day done with you. That is why it means most to people on their own, such as a parent whose children live away, a student in a hostel or a partner travelling for work.

Make it yours

  • Name the day: "Long one today. You handled it. Sleep now."
  • Close a worry: "Reports can wait till morning. Good night."
  • For a child away from home: one piece of home news, then "sleep well".
  • For elders: a blessing asked for, not given. "Good night, Dadi. Keep me in your prayers."

Timing and length

Send it before the person is likely to be asleep, and keep it to a line or two. If they have had a hard day, do not open a new subject. A good night message should let a person put the phone down, not pick it up.

What to avoid

  • Long forwards with moral stories.
  • "Are you asleep?" Nobody can answer that well.
  • Good night messages to colleagues and acquaintances who have not invited that closeness.

Make it yours

  • After a hard day, name it: "Today was heavy. Sleep now." People feel seen.
  • Keep it short. Nobody wants to read a paragraph with one eye open.
