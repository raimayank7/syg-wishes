Good morning wishes

A good morning message is a small way of saying "I thought of you first". These are short on purpose, so they are read and not just scrolled past.

For family and groups

  • Good morning! A new day has been delivered to your door. Open it gently.

  • Good morning, everyone. May today be easier than yesterday and kinder than you expect.

  • Rise and shine. Somebody somewhere is glad you woke up today. Good morning!

  • Good morning! Drink some water, stretch a little and be gentle with yourself today.

  • Good morning. Today does not have to be perfect. It only has to be lived well.

  • A fresh morning, a clean page. Write one good line on it today. Good morning!

  • Good morning, family. Wherever each of you is today, travel safe and eat on time.

For friends

  • Good morning! The tea is hot and the gossip is pending. Call me when you are awake.

  • Good morning, lazy. The sun has already clocked in. Your turn.

  • Good morning! May your coffee be strong and your Monday be short.

  • Wake up, my friend. The world needs your nonsense today. Good morning!

  • Good morning! Small reminder: you have survived every bad day so far. Today will be fine.

For someone special

  • Good morning. You were my last thought at night and my first one today.

  • Good morning, love. I hope your day is as lovely as the way you say my name.

  • Woke up, smiled, and then remembered why. Good morning to you.

  • Good morning. Have a good day, and save a little of your evening for me.

For colleagues

  • Good morning, team. Clear goals, calm minds, good work. Let us have a productive day.

  • Good morning! Wishing you a smooth day with short meetings and quick approvals.

  • Good morning. One task at a time, and the list will give up before we do.

Why most good morning messages are ignored

Because they are forwards: a picture of a flower, a borrowed line, sent to forty people at once. The receiver knows it took no thought. A good morning written to one person, with their name or their day in it, is read and answered. One real message is worth a month of forwards.

Make it yours

  • Mention their day: "Good morning. Interview at eleven, is it not? You will do well."
  • Ask a small question: "Good morning, Papa. Did the plumber come?"
  • Share your morning: "Good morning from a very foggy bus stop."
  • Carry on yesterday's talk: "Good morning. Did the baby finally sleep?"

Family and elders' groups

In many family groups a morning greeting is the way elders say "I am well, are you?" Reply to it, even briefly. If you are the one sending, change the line daily, add a blessing, and skip the heavy pictures that fill everyone's phone. For parents living alone, a personal good morning from a son or daughter does more than anything posted in a group.

When not to send

  • In office and school groups, unless that is the group's custom.
  • Before six in the morning. Not everyone silences their phone.
  • Every single day to someone who never answers. Take the hint kindly.

Make it yours

  • Do not send the same line to the same group every day. Rotate, or skip a day.
  • A good morning with a question ("Big day today?") gets a reply. A forward does not.
  • For family groups, a wish with a blessing for elders is always welcome.
