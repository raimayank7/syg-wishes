Self-introduction speech

A ready speech for a new student or employee, on the first day. Use the short version when you have about a minute, and the full version when you have about three. Both are our own writing; change them freely.

1 minute3 minutesहिंदीMake it yours

Words in [square brackets] are for you to replace with your own names and details.

Short speech (1 minute)

63 words · about 1 minute at a calm pace

Good morning, everyone. My name is [Your Name]. I come from [Town]. I have completed my [class or degree] from [School or College]. I enjoy [one hobby], and I am always ready to learn something new. I am a little nervous today, but I am happy to be here. I look forward to knowing all of you and to working together. Thank you.

Full speech (3 minutes)

220 words · about 2 minutes at a calm pace

Good morning, everyone. Thank you for giving me a minute to introduce myself.

My name is [Your Name], and most people call me [short name]. I come from [Town], which is known for [one thing about the town]. I did my schooling at [School] and completed my [degree] from [College] in [year].

About my work: for the last [number] years I have worked at [previous place], where I looked after [your main task]. The part of the work I enjoy most is [something specific, for example solving a customer's problem or making a messy sheet clean]. If this is your first job or first year, say instead what you enjoyed most in your studies and one project you are proud of.

Outside work, I like [one or two hobbies]. On a free Sunday you will probably find me [doing what]. I am also learning [a new skill], slowly but steadily.

I am new here, so I will have many questions in the first few weeks: where things are, how things are done and who does what. Please be patient with me. In return, I promise to learn fast, to be on time and to help wherever I can.

I am very happy to join [class, team or company name], and I look forward to knowing each of you. Thank you.

Make it yours

A ready speech gets you started; your own details make people listen. Before you use this one:

  • Fill every bracket with a true detail before you speak. A blank in a self-introduction is worse than a short one.
  • Sixty seconds is the right length unless you are asked for more.
  • Do not begin with "Myself"; say "My name is" or "I am".
  • Add one true detail from your own school, office or family. It is the line people will remember.
  • Read it aloud with a timer and cut what you do not need. The speech timer shows the time for any draft.
