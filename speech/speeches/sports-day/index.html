Sports Day speech

A ready speech for the Sports Captain or a student, to open the Sports Day. Use the short version when you have about a minute, and the full version when you have about three. Both are our own writing; change them freely.

1 minute3 minutesMake it yours

Words in [square brackets] are for you to replace with your own names and details.

Short speech (1 minute)

78 words · about 1 minute at a calm pace

Good morning, respected Chief Guest, Principal, teachers and my fellow athletes. Welcome to our Annual Sports Day. Sport teaches us what no book can: to play by the rules, to work as a team and to rise after a fall. Today some of us will win medals and all of us will win experience. Let us compete with all our strength and shake hands at the end. I wish every participant the very best. Let the games begin!

Full speech (3 minutes)

253 words · about 2 minutes at a calm pace

Good morning, respected Chief Guest, respected Principal, teachers, parents and my dear friends. As the Sports Captain of [School Name], it is my privilege to welcome you to our Annual Sports Day.

For weeks this ground has been busy from early morning. We have run laps till our legs ached, practised the march-past till our arms moved together, and argued about which house will lift the trophy. Today all that practice meets its test.

People often say that sport is only for fitness. I believe it is also a classroom, with lessons that do not come in any textbook. On the track we learn that there are no shortcuts; the stopwatch cannot be fooled. In a relay we learn to trust someone else with the baton. In a defeat we learn the hardest lesson of all: to congratulate the winner and come back stronger.

To my fellow athletes, I have three requests. Play hard, because you owe your best to your house. Play fair, because a medal won by cheating weighs nothing. And play happily, because we will remember this day long after we forget who came first.

To the students in the stands: your cheering is half our energy. Cheer for your own house, but clap for every good effort, whichever colour it wears.

I thank our Physical Education teachers, who trained us with patience and a very loud whistle, and the staff who marked this field. I now request our Chief Guest to declare the meet open. Let the games begin!

Make it yours

A ready speech gets you started; your own details make people listen. Before you use this one:

  • Speak a little faster and louder than usual; it is an outdoor event with energy.
  • Hold the microphone close. Wind and open ground swallow sound.
  • End on "Let the games begin!" and stop. Do not add anything after it.
  • Add one true detail from your own school, office or family. It is the line people will remember.
  • Read it aloud with a timer and cut what you do not need. The speech timer shows the time for any draft.
