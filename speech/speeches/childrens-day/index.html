Children's Day speech by a teacher

A ready speech for a teacher or the Principal, on 14 November. Use the short version when you have about a minute, and the full version when you have about three. Both are our own writing; change them freely.

1 minute3 minutesMake it yours

Short speech (1 minute)

73 words · about 1 minute at a calm pace

Good morning, my dear children. Today, 14 November, is your day. We celebrate it on the birthday of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, our first Prime Minister, who loved children dearly. You are the reason this school exists. Your questions keep us young and your laughter fills these corridors. Be curious, be kind and be brave enough to make mistakes. We teachers are proud of each one of you. Enjoy your day. Happy Children's Day!

Full speech (3 minutes)

269 words · about 2 minutes at a calm pace

Good morning, my dear children. On most days you sit and listen to us. Today the day belongs to you, and I am happy to stand here only to tell you how special you are.

In India, Children's Day is celebrated on 14 November, the birthday of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, the first Prime Minister of our country. Children fondly called him Chacha Nehru. He believed that the children of today will make the India of tomorrow, and that the way we bring them up decides the future of the nation.

Let me tell you what we teachers see when we look at you. We do not see roll numbers and marks. We see a girl who shares her tiffin without being asked. We see a boy who fell in the race, got up and still finished it. We see a quiet child at the back who draws beautifully. Every one of you carries something that no examination can measure.

I have three small wishes for you today. First, stay curious. Ask "why" and "how", even when the answer is not in the textbook. Second, be kind. The world has plenty of clever people; it needs more kind ones. Third, do not be afraid of mistakes. A notebook with no crossings usually belongs to someone who did not try anything new.

And a request: childhood is short. Play in the sun, read stories, climb, sing and get your uniform dirty sometimes. The phone can wait. These years will not come again.

On behalf of all your teachers, I wish you a very happy Children's Day. Now, let the fun begin!

Make it yours

A ready speech gets you started; your own details make people listen. Before you use this one:

  • Speak warmly and slowly; small children are listening too.
  • Replace the three examples with real things you have seen your own students do, without naming them.
  • Keep it short. On this day the children want the programme, not the speech.
  • Add one true detail from your own school, office or family. It is the line people will remember.
  • Read it aloud with a timer and cut what you do not need. The speech timer shows the time for any draft.
