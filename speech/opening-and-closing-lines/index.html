Opening and closing lines for speeches and anchoring

The first line of a speech decides whether people listen, and the last line decides what they remember. Here are 43 lines of our own to start you off: openings, closings and the short links an anchor needs between items. None of them is a quotation from anyone; you may use and change them freely. Tap Copy to take a line.

Choose a line that suits your audience and your own way of speaking. A playful line in the mouth of a serious person sounds borrowed. Say the line aloud twice. If it does not feel like you, change the words until it does.

Opening lines

To begin any school function

  • Good morning, respected Principal, respected teachers and my dear friends.
  • A very warm welcome to each one of you on this special day.
  • It is a joy to see this hall full of familiar faces and proud parents.

To begin with a question

  • Let me begin with a question: what is the one thing you would never forget about your school?
  • How many of us here have ever said thank you to the person who rings the school bell?
  • If you had only two minutes to say something important, what would you say? I have two minutes, so here is mine.

To begin with a picture

  • Think of a lamp. It gives light, but only because someone filled it with oil.
  • Picture this ground thirty years ago: no building, only a few teachers and a dream.
  • Imagine a classroom with no teacher. Now you know why we are here today.

To begin an evening programme

  • Good evening, ladies and gentlemen, and welcome to an evening we have been rehearsing for weeks.
  • The lights are on, the curtain is ready and so are our performers. Welcome!
  • Thank you for giving us your evening. We promise to make it worth the traffic.

To begin a farewell

  • Goodbyes are never easy, so today let us call this a thank-you party instead.
  • They say time flies. Looking at this batch, I can tell you it also grows taller.
  • We have gathered to do something difficult: to say goodbye to people we would rather keep.

To begin at a family function

  • Namaste and a very warm welcome to our family and friends. You have made our day by coming.
  • Our home feels bigger today because all of you are in it.
  • Before the food steals your attention, may I have two minutes?

Closing lines

To end a speech

  • With these words, I thank you all for listening so patiently.
  • Let us leave this hall with one small promise, and let us keep it.
  • I began with a question. I hope each of us goes home with an answer. Thank you.

To end a national day speech

  • Let us be citizens that the flag can be proud of. Jai Hind!
  • Freedom was their gift to us. Let responsibility be our gift to the country. Jai Hind!

To end a farewell

  • Go well, do well, and come back to tell us about it.
  • This is not goodbye. It is only "see you soon", said with wet eyes.
  • Wherever you go, a part of this place goes with you, and a part of you stays here.

To end a function as anchor

  • On that happy note, we come to the end of our programme. Thank you for being a wonderful audience.
  • The stage lights will go off, but we hope the memories stay on. Good night, and travel safely.
  • Thank you, one and all. Dinner is waiting, and so, I think, are you.

To end a family function

  • Thank you for your love and blessings. Please do not leave without eating!
  • May we meet again soon, for another happy reason.

Links for anchors

After a dance

  • What energy! The stage is still shaking. Thank you, performers.
  • That was colour, rhythm and joy in three minutes. A big hand once more!

After a song

  • A voice like that makes a hall fall silent. Thank you for that lovely song.
  • Some songs are heard by the ears and some by the heart. That was the second kind.

After a speech

  • Thank you for those thoughtful words. They have given us much to think about.
  • Short, clear and from the heart. Thank you.

Before a prize distribution

  • Hard work has a habit of being noticed. It is time to honour our achievers.
  • Now for the moment that many parents have their cameras ready for.

To fill a pause

  • While the stage is being set, let us give a round of applause to the teachers working behind the curtain.
  • A short pause, friends. Good things take a minute to arrange.

To invite the chief guest to speak

  • We have heard from the students. Now it is time to hear from experience. May I invite our Chief Guest to address us.
  • The moment we have been waiting for: a few words from our Chief Guest.

How to write your own opening line

  1. Start from the occasion. What is special about this day or these people? Say that, simply.
  2. Use a question, a picture or a tiny story. Each makes the listener's mind do something, and an active mind pays attention.
  3. Keep it under twenty words. A long first sentence loses the room before you have begun.
  4. Never open with an apology or with "I have been asked to say a few words".

How to write your own closing line

  1. Return to your opening. If you began with a question, answer it. If you began with a picture, complete it. The speech then feels whole.
  2. Give the audience one thing to do or to remember.
  3. End on a strong word. "Jai Hind", "thank you", "congratulations": do not trail off into "so, yes, that is it".
