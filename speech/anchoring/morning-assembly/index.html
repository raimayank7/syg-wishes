Anchoring script for the morning assembly

For one student conducting the daily school assembly. 8 parts, about 185 words of anchor lines.

The morning assembly is the first speaking stage most students ever get. The order differs a little from school to school, but it usually has a prayer, the pledge, a thought for the day, news headlines, a short talk, announcements and the National Anthem. Here are clear lines for the student who conducts it. Keep the whole assembly within the time your school allows.

Words in [square brackets] are for you to replace. Lines in (round brackets) are directions, not to be read aloud.

The script

Opening

Good morning, respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends. I am [Name] of class [class and section]. Today is [day], the [date]. Our class is conducting today's assembly.

Prayer

Let us begin the day by remembering the Almighty. School, stand in attention. Join your hands and close your eyes for the morning prayer.

Pledge

Thank you. Now [Name] will lead us in the pledge. School, please stretch your right hand forward and repeat.

Thought for the day

A good thought is like a good breakfast for the mind. For the thought of the day, I call upon [Name].

News

Let us know what is happening around us. [Name] will read today's news headlines: national, international and sports.

Short talk

Today's topic is [topic]. [Name] will speak to us about it for two minutes. Please listen carefully.

Birthdays and announcements

We wish a very happy birthday to those born today. May they please come forward. (After the wishes) I now request [teacher's name] to make the announcements for the day.

Close

School, stand in attention for the National Anthem. (After the anthem) Thank you. Have a great day ahead. Classes will now move to their rooms, starting from [side].

Tips for the day

  • Give commands such as "School, attention" loudly, with a short pause before and after.
  • Check the date, the day and every speaker's name before you step up.
  • If a speaker is absent, move to the next item without comment.
  • Print two copies in large type. Paper does not run out of battery.

How to adapt this script

No two functions have the same items. Treat each part above as a block: remove the blocks you do not need, repeat the "introducing an item" block for each performance, and put them in the order of your running sheet. Then write one fresh linking sentence for each item after you have seen its rehearsal; a line that fits the actual performance is worth more than any ready line. The guide how to anchor a function explains running orders, links and what to do when something goes wrong, and opening and closing lines has more lines to choose from.
