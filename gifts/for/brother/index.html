Gift ideas for your brother

With brothers, usefulness and humour go further than sentiment. Think of what he is into this year: a sport, a game, a bike, a new job.

  • A handwritten letter

    Say three specific things you are grateful for. It is kept longer than any object.

    TokenHandmade

  • A framed photograph of the two of you

    Choose a photo they have not seen for years. Print it larger than you think.

    TokenPersonal

  • A box of their favourite sweets

    Find out the exact shop and the exact sweet. Precision is the gift.

    TokenFood

  • A good pen

    For someone who still writes by hand. Have the name engraved if the shop offers it.

    TokenPractical

  • A book you loved

    Write a line on the first page saying why you chose it for them.

    TokenPersonal

  • A jar of homemade pickle, jam or laddus

    Label it with the date and what is inside. Homemade food carries your time in it.

    TokenHandmade

  • A photo collage printed as a poster

    Collect pictures from friends and family first, then join them in any collage maker and print the result.

    TokenHandmade

  • A keychain with a name or a small photo

    Useful every day and impossible to have too many of.

    TokenPersonal

  • A desk organiser or good notebook

    Neutral, useful and right for the office.

    TokenPractical

  • A rakhi with sweets and a small gift

    For brothers far away, post it two weeks early.

    TokenTraditional

  • A homemade cake or batch of cookies

    Bake their favourite flavour and pack it in a tin they can keep.

    TokenHandmade

  • A good umbrella or raincoat

    Unromantic and thanked for every monsoon.

    TokenPractical

  • A steel water bottle or lunch box

    Sturdy, daily and impossible to dislike.

    TokenPractical

  • A board game for the whole family

    Choose one that grandparents and children can play together.

    ModestPractical

  • A cricket bat, football or badminton set

    Outdoor toys get used the most. Check the right size for the child's age.

    ModestPractical

  • A shirt or kurta

    Check the size from a shirt in his cupboard, not from memory.

    ModestPractical

  • A day out together

    A film, lunch and a walk. Put the phone away for the day.

    ModestExperience

  • A toolkit for the house

    Every new home needs a hammer, a screwdriver set and a measuring tape.

    ModestPractical

  • Money in an envelope (shagun)

    Welcome on almost every family occasion, and the giver never has to guess a size or a taste. By custom the amount ends in one.

    ModestTraditional

  • A gift card for a shop they use

    Less personal, never wasted. Good when you live far away.

    ModestPractical

  • A phone cover, stand or power bank

    Check the phone model first.

    ModestPractical

  • A fitness band, skipping rope or yoga mat

    Only for someone who has started exercising, never as a hint.

    ModestPractical

  • Sports gear for their game

    A racket grip, a pair of gloves, a jersey of their team.

    ModestPractical

  • A grooming kit

    A good razor, trimmer or a set of skin care basics.

    ModestPractical

  • A scarf, tie or pair of cufflinks

    Accessories need no size.

    ModestPractical

  • A handbag or wallet

    Look at the one in use now, and choose the same style in better quality.

    GenerousPractical

  • A wrist watch

    A classic gift for a milestone: a first job, a wedding, a retirement.

    GenerousPractical

  • Perfume or attar

    Buy the one they already wear, or go together to choose.

    GenerousPersonal

  • Tickets to a concert, play or match

    For the fan of a band, a team or the theatre. Go with them.

  • A class or workshop

    Pottery, cooking, music, dance, photography: something they said they would try one day.

    GenerousExperience

    For someone starting a new job, a new city or a long-planned journey.

    GenerousPractical

    Find out whether they prefer wired or wireless, and what they listen on.

Your shortlist

How to choose for your brother

  • Ask his friends or his partner what he has been talking about.
  • For a younger brother, something that makes him look sharp at college or at a first job is well received.
  • An old photo of the two of you, framed, works at any age.

About the spending levels

This site does not quote prices, because prices change with the city, the shop and the year. Each idea is marked with one of four levels instead:

  • Token: A small gesture: the cost of a snack or a few cups of tea.
  • Modest: An everyday gift for friends and family.
  • Generous: A special gift for someone close or a big occasion.
  • Grand: A once-in-years gift, often given by a family together.

Questions people ask

What is a good gift for your brother?

Start from what the person does every day and what they would not buy for themselves. On this page, "A handwritten letter" and "A day out together" are two ideas that suit most people.

What if I have very little to spend?

Choose the Token level in the filter. Handmade and personal gifts, such as a handwritten letter, cost little and are often the ones remembered.
