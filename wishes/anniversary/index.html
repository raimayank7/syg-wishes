Anniversary wishes

An anniversary wish is a small thank you for staying. Choose a wish for your own partner, for your parents or for a couple you know, and add the number of years if you know it.

For your husband or wife

  • Happy anniversary, my love. We have built a life out of small days, and I would not trade one of them.

  • Happy anniversary to the person who knows my moods and stays anyway. Thank you for choosing me, again and again.

  • Another year of sharing one blanket and many dreams. Happy anniversary, partner.

  • We argue about the fan speed and agree about everything that matters. Happy anniversary to us.

  • Happy anniversary. I married my best friend, and somehow the friendship keeps getting better.

  • Years ago I said yes. I would say it faster today. Happy anniversary, my dear.

For parents

  • Happy anniversary, Mummy and Papa. Your marriage is the first love story I ever saw, and still the best one.

  • Happy anniversary to my parents. You showed us that love is mostly patience, tea and showing up.

  • Mom and Dad, thank you for building the home we all run back to. Happy anniversary.

  • Happy anniversary to the couple who taught us how to disagree and still eat dinner together.

  • Wishing my parents a very happy anniversary. May you keep teasing each other for many more years.

For a couple you know

  • Happy anniversary to a lovely couple. May your home always have laughter in it.

  • Congratulations on another year together. You two make it look easy, and we know it is not.

  • Happy anniversary! Wishing you both good health, good food and many more trips together.

  • To a couple who are better together: happy anniversary. Here is to the years ahead.

  • Happy first anniversary! The first year is the trailer. The full film is going to be wonderful.

  • Happy silver jubilee! Twenty five years of standing by each other is worth every bit of the celebration.

Three kinds of anniversary wish

  • To your own husband or wife: the most personal message of the year. One thing they did, one thing you love, one thing you look forward to.
  • To your parents: thanks for the home their marriage made. Children's wishes are the ones parents read out to relatives.
  • To another couple: address both by name, and wish them as a pair.

Make it yours

Put the number in: the years, the houses, the cities, the cups of tea. "Twenty two years, three cities, one scooter and then a car, and you still save me the last piece." Numbers and objects carry love better than adjectives do. For a milestone such as the twenty-fifth or the fiftieth, add one sentence about what the couple has built that others can see: the family, the friendships, the open house.

When the day is tender

If a couple has had a hard year through illness, loss or money trouble, choose a quiet wish about staying together, and leave out jokes. If one partner has died, the date still matters to the one who remains. A message that says "thinking of you both today" is a kindness few people remember to send.

What to avoid

  • Jokes about suffering each other, unless you are certain both will laugh.
  • Wishing one partner and forgetting the other.
  • Asking about children.

Make it yours

  • Mention the number of years. "Twenty five years" says more than "many years".
  • For your own spouse, name one thing they did this year that you noticed.
  • For parents, say what their marriage taught you.
