Engagement wishes

An engagement is the happy news before the big day. Keep the wish short and glad, and save the long blessing for the wedding.

Wishes

  • Congratulations on your engagement! The best chapter is about to start. So happy for you both.

  • You two finally made it official. Congratulations! Now the real planning, and the real fun, begins.

  • Wishing you both joy as you step towards your wedding day. Congratulations on your engagement.

  • Heard the good news and could not stop smiling. Congratulations to you and your partner!

  • A ring, a promise and two happy families. Congratulations on your engagement.

  • Congratulations, my friend! You found someone who makes you smile like that. Hold on to them.

  • To my brother and his bride to be: welcome to the family, and good luck with him.

  • To my sister and her groom to be: he is a lucky man, and he knows it. Congratulations!

  • Best wishes on your engagement. May the months of planning be as happy as the day itself.

  • Congratulations on your engagement. Wishing you both a smooth road to the wedding and a happy life after it.

  • Two families, one happy evening. Congratulations on the engagement, and blessings to you both.

  • The ring looks lovely, and you two look happier still. Congratulations.

  • So this is the person who has been making you smile at your phone. Congratulations to you both.

  • Congratulations on your daughter's engagement. May the two families share many happy occasions.

Short and glad

An engagement wish is the first of several you will send this couple, so keep it light. Congratulate them, say you are happy, and leave the long blessings for the wedding. If you know the story of how they met or how the families agreed, one line of it makes the wish personal.

Make it yours

  • To a close friend: "You said you would never find anyone who likes old songs as much as you do. I am told he hums in the kitchen. Congratulations to you both."
  • To a sibling: thanks to the new member for taking them on, said with a smile.
  • To a colleague or a neighbour's child: both names if you know them, and blessings to both families.

For the families

In many homes the engagement is as much the families' occasion as the couple's. A wish to the parents, "Congratulations on Simran's engagement, and blessings to both families", is good manners and is much appreciated, especially from elders and neighbours.

What to avoid

  • "When is the wedding?" in the same breath. They will tell you.
  • Comments on how long it took or how young they are.
  • Sharing the news or photographs before the family does.

Make it yours

  • Name both people if you can.
  • A light joke works for close friends. For a colleague or a relative you rarely meet, keep it simple and warm.
