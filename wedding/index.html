The first five decisions

  1. Budget: a total that both families agree on.
  2. Guest count: a number for each side.
  3. Date: two or three options, checked with elders and the priest.
  4. Venue: booked for that date.
  5. Functions: which ones you will hold, and on which days.

Everything else, from the menu to the mehndi, is easier once these five are settled. The checklist takes it from there.

How to use this site

Twelve to six months before. Enter the date above and open the checklist: it turns the date into deadlines. Set the total in the budget planner, start the guest list, and take the vendor questions to every meeting with a venue, caterer or photographer. Thinking of something simpler? Read how to plan a small wedding first.

Three months to one month before. Plan the haldi, mehndi and sangeet, settle the menu, arrange rooms and pick-ups for outstation guests, and make the duty sheet in who does what.

The last week and the day. Share the timeline, hand the photographer the family photo list and pack the wedding day bag.

Afterwards. The first month covers thank-yous, payments and paperwork, including registration.

Private by design

The checklist ticks, budget figures and guest names you enter are kept in your own browser, on your own phone or computer. They are not sent to us, and there is no account to create. That also means they do not travel between devices by themselves: plan on the device you use most, and print or copy a list when you want to share it with the family.

Questions people ask

Is this site free?

Yes. Every list and tool is free, with no login.

Where are my lists saved?

In your own browser, on your own device. Nothing you type is sent to us.

Does it suit every kind of Indian wedding?

The planning tools suit any wedding. The ritual pages cover eight traditions in general terms; your own family's customs come first.

Can two people share the same checklist?

Each phone keeps its own ticks. To work together, print the checklist or copy the guest list into a shared sheet.
