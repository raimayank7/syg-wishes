Wedding day timeline

A written, hour-by-hour plan is the cheapest thing you can add to a wedding and the most useful. It tells the makeup artist when to finish, the driver when to leave and the caterer when to open the counters. Here are two sample plans to adapt. The times are examples, not rules: your priest's muhurat and your venue decide the real ones.

Sample plan: evening wedding with baraat

TimeWhat happens
8:00 amBreakfast. Light rituals at home, such as the chooda ceremony or a puja.
11:00 amPhotographer arrives for family pictures at home.
1:00 pmLunch. The bride starts makeup and hair.
4:00 pmGroom gets ready. Sehra bandi with the family.
5:00 pmBride is ready. Portraits before the guests arrive.
6:30 pmBaraat leaves. Guests begin to arrive at the venue.
7:30 pmBaraat arrives. Milni and welcome.
8:15 pmJaimala on the stage, followed by photographs with guests.
8:30 pmDinner opens.
10:30 pmCouple and close family eat.
11:30 pmPheras in the mandap, at the time given by the priest.
1:30 amVidaai.

Sample plan: morning wedding

TimeWhat happens
5:00 amBride starts makeup and hair.
6:30 amGroom and family get ready.
7:30 amFamily reaches the hall or gurdwara. Tea and breakfast for early guests.
8:30 amWelcome of the groom's party. Opening rituals.
9:30 amMain ceremony at the auspicious time: the thali, the laavan or the vows.
10:30 amBlessings and photographs with guests.
12:00 noonWedding lunch.
2:00 pmFarewell of the bride, or rest before an evening reception.
7:00 pmReception, where one is held.

How to write your own

  1. Start from the fixed point. Write the time of the main ritual first, then work backwards.
  2. Ask each vendor how long they need. Bridal makeup and hair commonly take three hours or more. Decoration needs the hall for several hours before guests arrive.
  3. Add a buffer of 20 to 30 minutes before every move from one place to another. Baraats are never early.
  4. Give the couple a meal time and name the person who will make sure they eat.
  5. Plan the photographs: portraits before guests arrive, family groups right after the garlands, with a list of groups in the photographer's hand.
  6. Share one page with both families, the venue manager and every vendor, with phone numbers at the bottom.

Rules the day will thank you for

  • Check the venue's closing time for music and for the hall itself, and plan the end of the function around it.
  • Keep one car and driver free for emergencies and forgotten things.
  • Elders and small children need to eat on time. Open one counter early for them.
  • Decide in advance who collects the gifts and envelopes, and where they are kept.
