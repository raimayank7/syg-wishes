Wedding

Planning the haldi, mehndi and sangeet

The functions before the wedding are where the two families actually meet, laugh and get tired. They need less money than the wedding day and more thought, because they are held close together and the same few people organise all of them. This guide covers the three most common: haldi, mehndi and sangeet. The names change by community (maiyan or vatna, pithi, gaye holud, manjha), but the planning is much the same.

Choosing the order and the days

A common pattern for a wedding on a Sunday evening:

  • Friday evening: mehndi at the bride's home, with the women of both families.
  • Saturday morning: haldi, separately in each home or together.
  • Saturday evening: sangeet for both families.
  • Sunday: the wedding.

Three rules shape the order:

  1. Mehndi needs time. The bride's henna takes hours to apply and deepens in colour over the next day or two, so it comes first. It should never be on the morning of the wedding.
  2. Haldi is messy. Hold it in the morning, in old clothes, with time to bathe and rest afterwards.
  3. Leave a quiet gap before the wedding. A sangeet that ends at two in the morning before a morning wedding leaves everyone exhausted, the bride most of all.

Short of days? Combine. Mehndi and sangeet work well as one evening: the henna artists sit in one corner while the singing and dancing goes on. Haldi and mehndi on the same day also works, haldi first.

Haldi

What it is: relatives apply a paste of turmeric to the bride and the groom, with songs and teasing. Who comes: close family and friends. It is the smallest and warmest function.

  • Place: a courtyard, terrace or lawn that can be washed. Turmeric stains floors, walls and sofas. Cover what cannot be moved.
  • Things to arrange: the paste (turmeric with sandalwood, gram flour, curd or oil, as your family makes it), a low stool, flowers, old towels, and water for washing hands.
  • Clothes: yellow is traditional, and whatever is worn will be stained for good. Tell guests.
  • Skin: if the bride or groom has sensitive skin, test the paste on the arm a few days before, and keep it away from the eyes.
  • Photographs: morning light is the best of the whole wedding. Even if there is no photographer, give one cousin the job.

Mehndi

What it is: henna is applied to the bride's hands and feet and to the women of the family. Who comes: women of both sides, and increasingly everyone.

  • The artist: book early in the wedding season and ask to see photographs of the artist's own work. Agree in advance whether the price covers only the bride or guests too, and how many helpers will come.
  • Time: full bridal mehndi can take three to five hours. Start the bride early so that she is free to enjoy the rest of the evening. She will need someone to feed her and hold her phone.
  • Seating: low seating with bolsters for the bride, and chairs with a small table for guests.
  • Henna: ask the artist for natural henna. Very dark "black henna" pastes can contain dyes that irritate the skin, so avoid them, and do a small test first if the bride has reacted to henna before.
  • Guests: give each guest a turn number, or the oldest aunts will wait the longest.

Sangeet

What it is: an evening of songs and dances by the two families. Once it was a gathering of women with a dholak. Today it may be a stage show. Both are right.

  • Decide the scale first: a dholak evening at home, or a venue with a stage, sound and a DJ. The second needs a sound check, a host and a running order.
  • Performances: six to eight short items are plenty. Limit each to three or four minutes, alternate the two families, and put the couple's item or the parents' item last.
  • Practice: start three to four weeks ahead. Relatives in other cities can learn from a video shared in the family group.
  • The host: one cheerful cousin from each side, with the list of items and the names in the right order.
  • Music: collect every track on one phone or pen drive, named in running order, and hand it to the DJ before the evening begins. Check the venue's rules on how late music may be played.
  • Food: serve snacks early and dinner by a fixed hour. Hungry guests leave before the best performance.

A running order for a combined mehndi and sangeet

  1. 5:00 pm: henna artists begin with the bride.
  2. 6:30 pm: guests arrive, snacks, henna for guests.
  3. 7:30 pm: welcome by the hosts, a traditional song by the elders.
  4. 7:45 pm: performances, in two sets with a short break.
  5. 9:00 pm: dinner opens.
  6. 9:30 pm: open dance floor.
  7. 11:00 pm: close.

Mistakes families make

  • Inviting the whole wedding list to every function. Smaller functions are cheaper and more fun. It is normal to invite only close family to the haldi.
  • No one in charge. Give each function one named person who is not the bride, the groom or their parents.
  • Forgetting the elders. Seat them where they can see and hear, away from the speakers, and serve them first.
  • Running late. Every function that overruns steals sleep from the wedding day.

Put each function, with its date, into the planning checklist, give each its own head in the budget planner, and mark in the guest list who is invited to which. For invitation messages, see haldi, mehndi and sangeet wording on the sister site.

Questions people ask

Which comes first, haldi or mehndi?

Either order is followed. Many families hold the mehndi first, because henna needs a day or two to darken, and the haldi the next morning.

How long does bridal mehndi take?

Often three to five hours for full hands and feet, depending on the design. Start early in the day or evening.

Can mehndi and sangeet be held together?

Yes. It is a common way to save a day: the henna artists work in one part of the venue while the music goes on.
