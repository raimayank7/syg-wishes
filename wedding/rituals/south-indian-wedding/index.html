South Indian Hindu wedding: rituals in order

Hindu weddings in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Kerala are usually held in the morning, at a muhurtham chosen by the priest. The central moment in most of them is the tying of the thali or mangalsutra. Each state and community has its own rituals; these are some of the best known.

RitualWhenWhat happens
NischayatharthamBefore the weddingThe engagement. The families exchange a written agreement, gifts and often rings.
Pellikuthuru and haldi ritualsA day or two beforeIn Telugu families the bride and the groom are anointed with turmeric and oils at their own homes. Similar rituals exist across the south.
Kashi yatraWedding morningIn Tamil Brahmin weddings, the groom pretends to leave for Kashi to become an ascetic, and the bride's father persuades him to marry instead.
OonjalWedding morningThe couple sit on a decorated swing while the women of the family sing and bless them.
KanyadaanamIn the mandapamThe bride's father gives her hand to the groom. In Karnataka the pouring of water over the joined hands is called dhare.
Jeelakarra bellamAt the muhurthamIn Telugu weddings the couple, separated by a cloth, place a paste of cumin and jaggery on each other's heads at the exact auspicious time.
Mangalya dharanam (thali)At the muhurthamThe groom ties the thali or mangalsutra around the bride's neck with three knots, to the sound of the nadaswaram and drums.
TalambraluAfter the thaliIn Telugu weddings the couple shower each other with rice mixed with turmeric, a playful moment.
SaptapadiAfter the thaliThe couple take seven steps together around or beside the sacred fire.
Sadya or wedding mealAfter the ceremonyA vegetarian feast served on banana leaves. In Kerala the ceremony itself is brief and the sadya is the great event.

Customs differ from family to family and from place to place. This is a general guide for guests and for couples learning about each other's traditions, not a rule book. Reviewed on 11 November 2026.

If you are a guest

  • The wedding is in the morning and the muhurtham is exact. Come early.
  • Guests bless the couple with turmeric-coloured rice (akshata) when the thali is tied.
  • Silk saris and veshtis or dhotis are the traditional dress. Footwear is left outside the hall.
  • On a banana leaf, the meal is eaten with the right hand. Fold the leaf towards you when you finish, as is the custom in many places.

Planning notes for the two families

The muhurtham rules the day

The priest fixes the muhurtham, often early in the morning, and the thali must be tied within it. Book the hall from the evening before, plan the bride's dressing from the small hours and arrange transport so that the close family arrives well ahead.

The priest's list

Ask the vadhyar or purohit for the full list of items: the thali and its thread, turmeric, kumkum, coconuts, betel leaves and nuts, flowers, akshata and the items for each homam. Decide which family brings what.

Music and the stage

The nadaswaram and thavil players are booked with the hall or separately, and they need to know the moment of the mangalya dharanam so that the music rises with it. Keep the stage clear enough for elders to bless the couple.

The meal

The wedding meal is served in batches on banana leaves in many communities. Agree with the caterer on the number of batches, who invites guests to each sitting and how breakfast is handled for early arrivals.

Questions people ask

What is the muhurtham?

The auspicious time fixed by the priest for the main ritual, usually the tying of the thali. It is observed to the minute.

Why three knots?

By custom the groom ties the first knot and his sister ties the other two in many communities. The meanings given to the three knots differ from family to family.
