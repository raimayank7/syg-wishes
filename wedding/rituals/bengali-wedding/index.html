Bengali wedding: rituals in order

A Bengali Hindu wedding, the biye, is known for its conch shells and ululation, the bride's red and white, and a series of gentle, graceful rituals. It takes place in the evening.

RitualWhenWhat happens
AashirbaadBefore the weddingElders of each family bless the bride and the groom with rice, grass and gifts.
Aiburo bhaatThe day beforeThe last grand meal each of them eats in their parents' home before marriage.
Dodhi mangalDawn of the wedding dayThe bride and the groom are each fed a small meal of curd and flattened rice before sunrise.
Gaye holudWedding morningTurmeric paste is applied to the groom, and the same turmeric is then sent to the bride's home with gifts.
Bor jatri and boronEveningThe groom's party arrives, and the bride's mother welcomes the groom.
Saat paak and shubho drishtiEveningThe bride, seated on a low stool carried by her brothers and hiding her face behind betel leaves, is taken around the groom seven times. She then lowers the leaves and the two look at each other.
Mala bodolEveningThe couple exchange garlands three times.
SampradanIn the mandapAn elder of the bride's family gives her hand to the groom.
Yagna and saptapadiIn the mandapThe couple circle the sacred fire and take the seven steps.
Sindoor daanEnd of the ceremonyThe groom applies sindoor to the bride's parting, and her head is covered with a new sari.
Bidaai and bou bhaatThe following daysThe bride leaves for her new home. At the bou bhaat she serves a meal to her new family, and a reception follows.

Customs differ from family to family and from place to place. This is a general guide for guests and for couples learning about each other's traditions, not a rule book. Reviewed on 11 November 2026.

If you are a guest

  • Expect the sound of conch shells and ululation (ulu) at every happy moment.
  • The wedding dinner is a serious matter. Fish is usually part of it.
  • Women often wear saris, and red and white are the bride's colours.
  • By old custom the bride's mother does not watch the main ceremony, though many families no longer follow this.

Planning notes for the two families

Two homes, two timetables

Much of a Bengali wedding happens separately in the two homes: the aiburo bhaat, the dodhi mangal before dawn and the gaye holud, where turmeric and gifts (tattva) travel from the groom's house to the bride's. Share one written timetable so both households know when the tattva will leave and arrive.

The lagna

The ceremony is held in the evening at the lagna fixed by the priest. Plan the arrival of the bor jatri, the groom's party, and its welcome well before that hour.

Things to arrange

The topor and mukut (the white headgear of the groom and the bride), the piri or wooden seat on which the bride is carried for the saat paak, betel leaves for the shubho drishti, garlands, the conch shells, and the brothers or cousins who will lift the piri.

The feast and the bou bhaat

The wedding dinner is planned with as much care as the ceremony. The bou bhaat, hosted by the groom's family a day or two later, needs its own caterer, guest list and invitation.

For the hour-by-hour plan of the day, see the wedding day timeline, and for jobs to hand out, who does what.

Questions people ask

What is shubho drishti?

The moment when the bride lowers the betel leaves from her face and the couple look at each other in front of everyone.

What is bou bhaat?

A meal at the groom's home after the wedding, where the bride serves food to her new family. It is often combined with the reception.
