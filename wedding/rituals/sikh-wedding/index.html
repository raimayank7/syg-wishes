Sikh wedding (Anand Karaj): rituals in order

The Sikh wedding ceremony is the Anand Karaj, which means "blissful union". It is held in the morning in the presence of the Guru Granth Sahib, usually in a gurdwara, and is simple and short. The functions around it are full of Punjabi music and colour.

RitualWhenWhat happens
Roka or thaka, and kurmaiBefore the weddingThe families confirm the match. At the kurmai (engagement), gifts and often rings are exchanged.
Chunni ceremonyBefore the weddingThe groom's family presents the bride with a red chunni and gifts.
Maiyan or vatnaA day or two beforeA turmeric paste is applied to the bride and the groom at their own homes.
Mehndi and sangeetA day or two beforeHenna, folk songs with the dholki, giddha and bhangra.
JaggoThe night beforeRelatives carry decorated pots with lamps on their heads and go singing through the neighbourhood.
Chooda and kalireWedding morningThe bride's maternal uncle gives her the red and white bangles (chooda). Golden kalire are tied to them.
Sehra bandi, baraat and milniWedding morningThe groom is dressed, the baraat reaches the gurdwara, and the families greet each other.
Anand Karaj and the laavanIn the gurdwaraThe couple sit before the Guru Granth Sahib. The four laavan, hymns composed by Guru Ram Das, are read and sung one by one, and the couple walk around the Guru Granth Sahib once during each.
Ardas, hukamnama and karah prashadEnd of the ceremonyThe congregation stands for the prayer, a verse is read from the Guru Granth Sahib, and karah prashad is shared.
Langar and doliAfter the ceremonyEveryone eats together in the langar. The bride then leaves her parents' home.

Customs differ from family to family and from place to place. This is a general guide for guests and for couples learning about each other's traditions, not a rule book. Reviewed on 11 November 2026.

If you are a guest

  • Cover your head inside the gurdwara, men and women alike. Scarves are usually kept at the entrance.
  • Remove shoes and wash hands before entering the hall.
  • Sit on the floor facing the Guru Granth Sahib. Do not point your feet towards it.
  • The ceremony is in the morning. Arrive on time, because it is not long.

Planning notes for the two families

The gurdwara

Book the gurdwara for the Anand Karaj well ahead and ask the granthi about the starting time, how long the kirtan will last and what the families should bring, such as the rumala sahib. The ceremony is held in the presence of the Guru Granth Sahib, so the gurdwara's own customs come first.

Head coverings and seating

Keep scarves and rumals ready at the door for guests who arrive without one. Elderly guests who cannot sit on the floor can usually be seated at the back or the side: ask the gurdwara in advance.

The morning timetable

Because the laavan take place in the morning, the baraat, milni and tea must run to time. Plan the make-up, the turban tying and the photographs backwards from the hour the ceremony begins.

Langar and the party

Decide whether guests will eat langar at the gurdwara, a lunch at a separate venue, or both. If there is an evening reception with music and dancing, it is held away from the gurdwara.

Questions people ask

How many laavan are there?

Four. The couple walk around the Guru Granth Sahib once during each laav.

What does Anand Karaj mean?

It means "blissful union" or "ceremony of joy". Sikh marriages can be registered under the Anand Marriage Act, 1909, where the state has made rules for it.
