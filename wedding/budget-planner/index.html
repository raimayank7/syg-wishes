How to build a wedding budget

  1. Fix the total first. Decide what the families can spend without borrowing, and write it in the box above.
  2. Count the guests. Most costs are per head, so the guest list and the budget must be made together.
  3. Collect real quotes. Ask two or three vendors for each large head and enter the middle figure as "planned".
  4. Keep a reserve. Add a head called "Reserve" for the things nobody thought of. There are always some.
  5. Record every payment in the "spent" column on the day you make it, including advances.

Costs that are easy to forget

  • Taxes and service charges on venue and catering quotes.
  • Tips for staff, drivers and musicians.
  • Extra plates when more guests arrive than were counted.
  • Travel and stay for the photographer, the makeup artist and the priest.
  • Alterations, blouse stitching and accessories.
  • Gifts and envelopes for relatives in rituals.
  • Generator fuel, extra hours of the hall and overtime of the DJ.

Questions people ask

How should the budget be divided?

There is no fixed rule, because families spend very differently. In most Indian weddings the venue and the food together are the largest part. Fill in real quotes for your city and guest count, and let the sheet show you the picture.

How can we reduce the cost?

The guest count is the strongest lever, because food, seating, gifts and rooms all rise with it. Next come the date (weekdays and off-season dates cost less) and the number of functions.

Are my figures private?

Yes. They are stored only in your browser and are never sent to us.
