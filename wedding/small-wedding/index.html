How to plan a small, simple wedding

A small wedding is not a lesser wedding. It is a different choice: fewer people, less noise, more time with each guest, and money kept for the life that follows. More couples and parents want one than say so aloud, because the hard part is not the planning. It is the conversation. This guide covers both.

Decide what "small" means for you

There is no fixed number. Think in circles:

  • Only the two households: parents, siblings and grandparents. A temple, gurdwara, church, mosque or registrar's office, then a meal.
  • Close family: add uncles, aunts and first cousins, and a handful of friends.
  • Family and friends: everyone who would truly be missed.

Choose the circle first, then count. If you start from a number, every name becomes an argument.

Agree between the two families first

A small wedding works only when both families want the same thing. Have this conversation before any guest is told anything:

  • Which circle are we inviting, on both sides?
  • Is it equal numbers, or equal rules? Families differ in size, so the same rule ("first cousins, yes; second cousins, no") is usually fairer than the same number.
  • Which functions will we hold? One ceremony and one meal is a complete wedding.
  • Will there be a larger reception later for those not invited?

Making the guest list kindly

  1. Draw the line by relationship, not by person. "All first cousins" offends nobody. "This cousin but not that one" offends for years.
  2. Apply the rule on both sides, and to friends: for example, only friends both of you have met.
  3. No exceptions without a reason you could say aloud.
  4. Tell people yourself. A phone call from a parent to an elder who is not invited does more than any card.

What to say to relatives

Most hurt comes from hearing the news from someone else. Say it early, plainly and warmly. Lines you can adapt:

  • "We are keeping the wedding very small, only the two households. We wanted you to hear it from us, and we would love your blessings."
  • "We have decided on a simple ceremony with immediate family. We will come to meet you soon after with sweets and photographs."
  • "हम शादी बहुत सादगी से, सिर्फ़ घर के लोगों के बीच कर रहे हैं। आपका आशीर्वाद चाहिए।"

Then do what you promised: visit, send photographs, or hold a simple tea for the wider family afterwards.

Where to hold it

  • A place of worship: many temples, gurdwaras, churches and mosques conduct weddings and have a hall for the meal. Ask about dates, timings and their rules.
  • At home: a courtyard, terrace or the largest room. A home wedding needs a plan for seating, the kitchen, parking and the neighbours, but it has a warmth no hall can match.
  • A restaurant or small banquet room: the simplest of all. The food and service are taken care of.
  • A registered marriage: a marriage before the Marriage Officer, with a family lunch afterwards. See marriage registration for how it works.

What to keep, what to drop

Keep the things that the couple and the parents will remember. Drop the things that exist mainly for the crowd.

  • Keep: the ceremony itself, done properly and unhurried. A good photographer for a few hours. A good meal. Clothes you feel happy in. One function, such as a haldi or mehndi at home, if the family wants it.
  • Simplify: decoration (fresh flowers and lights go a long way in a small space), the menu (six excellent dishes instead of thirty), the stage (a small wedding does not need one).
  • Drop without guilt: the long receiving line, return gifts for hundreds, a second caterer for late-night snacks, a band for a procession of twenty people, unless you want one.

The advantages you can use

  • Timing is easy. Thirty people can be on time. The ceremony can be at the hour the priest prefers.
  • Everyone can be in the photographs. Plan a picture with each family.
  • Everyone can speak. Ask a grandparent to say a blessing, or each side to tell one story at lunch.
  • You can sit and eat together, at one long table, with the couple in the middle instead of on a stage.

Planning still matters

Small weddings fail in small ways: nobody ordered the garlands, the photographer did not know the time, the priest's list was left at home. Use the same checklist, skip the lines that do not apply, and still give one person outside the couple charge of the day. A short timeline shared with every guest keeps even a twenty-person wedding calm.

Sharing it with those who were not there

  • Send a few good photographs and a short message to the wider family the same evening, before they see pictures elsewhere.
  • A video call during the ceremony lets distant grandparents and friends abroad watch. Give one cousin the phone and a tripod.
  • Carry sweets when you visit relatives afterwards. It is an old custom and it still works.

Questions people ask

How many guests is a small wedding?

There is no fixed number. It usually means inviting by circle: only the two households, or close family and a few friends, instead of everyone the families know.

How do we tell relatives they are not invited?

Early, personally and warmly. A phone call from the parents, explaining that the wedding is limited to the immediate family and asking for blessings, prevents most hurt.

Can a small wedding still have traditional rituals?

Yes. The rituals need the couple, the families and the priest, not a crowd. Many families find the ceremony more meaningful with fewer people.
